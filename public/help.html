How to use EKA Builder

Describe a business, pick the designs you like, and get a fast, finished website you can change by asking the AI or by hand.

Getting started

On Windows

  1. Install Node.js LTS from nodejs.org (Next, Next, Finish).
  2. Download the builder as a ZIP, right-click it, choose Extract All, and open the extracted folder. Don't run it from inside the ZIP.
  3. Double-click start-windows.bat. The first start installs what it needs, then opens the builder in your browser.
  4. The first time, a random password is created and shown in the window. It's also saved in settings.txt.

To update later, download the new ZIP and double-click update-windows.bat in your builder folder. Your websites and settings are kept.

Logging in

Go to /admin on the builder's address and enter the password from settings.txt. Forgotten it? Open settings.txt in Notepad, change ADMIN_PASSWORD, save, and restart the builder.

Making a new website

Click + New website. There are four short steps:

  1. What are you making? Pick Business website, Online shop or Blog at the top of the first step, and tick any extras: a booking form, an enquiry / quote form or a blog. Each adds the pages it needs (Shop, Book, Blog), and you can change the pages before carrying on.
  2. Brief: the business name and what they do, plus optional logo, photos of the business, phone, email, address, opening hours, social links, brand colours, a style and which pages they need.
  3. Pages: a sentence or two about what goes on each page. Leave one blank and it's written from the business description.
  4. Homepage: you're shown a homepage design. Click Generate another for a different layout and flick between them. Type changes in the boxes for the whole site or any page.
  5. Other pages: each page gets the same treatment: Use this design, Generate another, or describe a change. When every page is picked, the site is built. Build the site now skips ahead; unpicked pages are designed automatically.
Photos are filled with tasteful placeholder pictures in the site's colours. In the editor they're marked “Placeholder”: double-click one to swap in a real photo.

Without an AI key, designs come from built-in templates using everything in the brief, and the changes you type are saved as a to-do list in the editor's AI tab.

The editor

The page is in the middle. On the left are five tabs: Add (blocks and ready-made sections), AI, Pages, Style (the whole site's look) and Site (address, hosting, forms). On the right are the settings for whatever you've selected.

  • Select anything by clicking it. Sections are the full-width bands; blocks are the pieces inside them.
  • Add by dragging from the Add tab onto the page, or select something and click a block to add it after.
  • Move by dragging the ⠿ handle on the selection bar, or with the ↑ ↓ buttons.
  • Edit text by double-clicking it on the page. Bold, italic and links work as you'd expect.
  • See it on other screens with the 🖥 ▭ 📱 buttons at the top.
  • Layers (left) lists everything on the page: click to select, ↑ ↓ to move, ◉ to hide.
  • Everything saves automatically. The ▾ next to undo lists recent changes so you can go back several at once; History keeps saved versions.

Section library & ready-made designs

Ready-made designs: in the first step of a new website, click Or start from a ready-made design for complete sites (restaurant, trades, salon, fitness, shop, professional services, photographer, B&B, charity, one page). With AI, every page is then written for the business.

Section library (Add tab, top): over 100 ready-made sections in categories, shown in the site's own colours and fonts. Click one to add it after the selected section. With Fill it in for this business ticked, the AI writes its text and finds photos.

On any section (right-hand panel): ✦ Fill with AI rewrites its text for the business; ✦ Restyle gives it a new design with the same words; ★ Save keeps it in My sections for any of your websites; ⇄ Share across pages puts the same section on several pages, and editing any copy changes them all.

Suggestions (AI tab): quick checks for what the site is missing, like reviews, an FAQ or Google descriptions, each with a one-click fix, plus AI ideas, Google descriptions for every page, and local pages ("Plumber in Leeds").

Styling

Style kits, logo colours and brand kits

At the top of the Style tab: style kits change the whole look in one click (fonts, colours, buttons, corners, spacing and animation), previewed on your own home page; tick Keep my colours to change everything else. 🎨 Colours from my logo picks the brand colours out of the logo. Brand kits save a site's look and logo to use on other websites, or when making a new one.

Shapes, icons, sliders and tabs

A section's Style tab has Shaped edges (waves, curves, slants, zig-zags…) that blend into the next section. Cards, list bullets and buttons can use any of over 250 icons (Choose icon…). The Slider block makes banner, review or photo carousels; the Tabs block makes tabs, pill buttons or an accordion.

Pictures and layout

Select an image and click Crop, zoom & focus point to choose the part that matters; the image settings also have shapes (circle, arch, blob…), filters and frames. In a section's Style tab you can set the first column's width and show the columns in reverse order on phones (picture first). Text can have its own size on tablets and phones. A Fix it button appears when text is hard to read.

The whole site: the Style tab on the left

Colour presets and colours, fonts, text sizes, corner rounding, content width, space between sections, buttons (size, shape, weight, capitals, shadow and hover animation), and an animation for sections as they scroll into view.

One item: the panel on the right

Select a block or section and use its three tabs:

  • Content (or Layout for sections): what it says and shows, columns, background choice.
  • Style: font, size, weight, capitals, italic, line height, letter spacing, text colour; background colour or gradient, background image or video, tint; border, corner radius and shadow; for buttons, size and hover animation.
  • Advanced: margin and padding (separately for desktop, tablet and phone), max width, min height, entrance animation with speed and delay, hover effect, and hiding on desktop, tablet or phone.
For phone- or tablet-only spacing, click 📱 or ▭ next to Margin or Padding (or at the top of the editor) and type the values. Empty boxes use the bigger screen's value.

Eye-catching effects

  • Text effects (a heading's or text's Style tab): words that blur or slide in one by one, a shine that sweeps across, a moving colour gradient, or a highlighter underline that draws itself.
  • Scrolling strip block: logos, reviews or selling points gliding across the page.
  • Count-up numbers: tick the option in a Stats block.
  • 3D tilt hover effect, and Slowly move the gradient for gradient backgrounds.

They're all done with CSS alone, so they don't slow the site down. Use them sparingly: one effect on the hero heading looks polished; one on every heading looks busy.

Animations play on the live site and in Preview, not while you're editing. Use ▶ Preview animation to see one. Visitors who've turned off motion on their device don't see them.

Header, footer & menu

Click the header or footer on the page, or use Edit header / Edit footer on the right when nothing is selected. Changes apply to every page.

  • Content: logo text or image, logo size (and on phones), the header button and its style, sticky header, menu links, footer text and links.
  • Style: background, text colour, font, border, shadow, padding, and the Menu links group: font, size, weight, capitals, spacing, colours (normal, hover, current page), hover effect (colour, underline, line above, pill) and position (right, centre, left).

Call / WhatsApp button: in Site → Business details, choose a contact button and it stays in the corner of the screen on phones, so visitors can ring or message the business in one tap.

The menu is built from your pages automatically. Untick that option in the header to write your own links. Want the logo somewhere else too? Add a Logo block.

Drop-down menus: open a page's settings and choose Show in the menu under another page (or, in a hand-made menu, tick In a drop-down under the item above). The header can have the logo on the left, centred above the menu, or in the middle of it, and can be see-through over a photo banner, turning solid as visitors scroll. An announcement bar above the header shows a short message with a link.

Images, galleries & video

Uploaded photos are resized and converted to WebP automatically, with a smaller copy for phones, so big phone photos don't slow the site down. Images load only when they're about to be seen.

Stock photos

With a free stock photo key, new designs get real photos that suit the business instead of placeholder artwork, and the image picker gets a stock photo search. The photos are free to use on websites.

  1. Make a free account at pixabay.com.
  2. While logged in, go to pixabay.com/api/docs. Your key is shown on that page (in the "Parameters" table, next to key).
  3. Paste it after PIXABAY_API_KEY= in settings.txt, save, and restart the builder.

A Pexels key (PEXELS_API_KEY=) works too, if you have one; Pexels has paused giving out new keys.

Galleries

Choose columns for desktop, tablets and phones, how many rows to show, the gap, the photo shape, grid or masonry layout, a hover effect, captions, and whether clicking a photo opens it full size (with next/previous arrows).

Background images

In a section's or block's Style tab: choose the image, a different one for phones, which part to keep in view, fill or fit, stay still while scrolling, or repeat as a pattern. Add a tint (a colour or gradient at any strength) to make text readable, an effect (fade in, slow zoom, zoom out, slow pan, parallax) and blur, black & white or brightness.

In new designs these are set for you: the AI picks which sections get a photo background and sets the tint, focus point and effect. It uses the business's own photos first (add them in the wizard's first step, with a note on what each shows), then a matching stock photo. The templates used without AI add a tint and slow zoom to their photo backgrounds too.

Background video

Upload an MP4 (best: 10–20 seconds, 1280px wide, a few MB) or paste a YouTube or Vimeo link. It plays silently on a loop. Phones show the background image instead unless you tick Play on phones too. Builder previews show the image, because previews block autoplay; the video plays on the live site.

Pop-ups, coming soon, sharing, visitors & domains

  • Pop-up (Site tab): an offer or newsletter signup, shown after a few seconds, when someone's about to leave, or halfway down the page, and only once per visitor every few days.
  • Coming soon (Site tab): while a site is being finished, visitors see a "coming soon" page in its style, with the newsletter signup. The "page not found" page is in the site's style too, with links to its pages.
  • Share picture (Site tab): the picture shown when the site is shared on Facebook or WhatsApp, made from a photo or the site's colours, with the logo and a headline.
  • 📈 Stats (dashboard): visitors, pages viewed, enquiries and where visitors came from, counted without cookies, and a monthly email report to the owner.
  • Connect & check the domain (Site tab → Address): the exact DNS settings for a customer's domain, and a live check that they're working.

Pages & Google

The Pages tab adds, renames, reorders and deletes pages. With nothing selected, the right-hand panel shows the page's name, web address, and the title and description Google shows in search results (aim for 120–155 characters).

Using AI

The AI designs new sites and makes changes you describe, for example “add a pricing section”, “make it feel more luxurious” or, with something selected, “make this punchier”. Every AI change can be undone.

Turning it on

  1. Create an account at console.anthropic.com, add a payment method, and create an API key.
  2. Open settings.txt, paste the key after ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=, save, and restart the builder.

You pay Anthropic for what you use. Designs and new sites use the strongest model so the first designs need few changes; changes use a faster, cheaper one and send back only what changed. Instructions and the approved design are cached between calls, so repeat reads cost a fraction of the price. Without a key everything else still works: designs come from templates and your requests become a to-do list in the AI tab.

What it costs: the AI this month button on the dashboard shows the estimated spend this month, by type of job and by website. Your Anthropic bill is the exact figure.

Forms: contact, enquiries & bookings

In the Add tab, Booking form and Enquiry form (under Ready-made sections) add a form already set up for the job; the Form block lets you build your own. For each form you choose:

  • What it's for: a contact message, an enquiry / quote request or a booking request. This sets the email subject and how it's labelled in the messages list.
  • Fields: short or long text, email, phone, date, time, number, a drop-down list (type the choices separated by commas, e.g. your services) and a tick box (e.g. "I agree to be contacted"). Tick Required for anything visitors must fill in.
  • Email the visitor a copy: they get a "we've received your booking request" email with what they sent. Booking copies say the booking isn't confirmed until the business replies.

Everything sent arrives in Site → Messages, enquiries & bookings, with tabs for bookings, enquiries and messages, a Reply button and a Done tick so nothing is forgotten. It's also emailed to the address in Site → Forms: on a customer's own hosting this always works; on the builder's own server it needs email set up (see Settings; most Linux servers can already send).

Booking forms collect requests; they don't block out times or take payment. The business confirms each booking by replying. Forms include spam protection.

Blog

Blog posts are written in the editor like pages. In the Pages tab, under Blog posts, click + New post (or ✦ Write a post with AI and say what it's about). In the right-hand panel set the title, date, a short summary, a cover photo and the author. New posts are drafts: untick Draft and publish the site to put a post live.

Posts appear wherever there's a Blog posts block (Add tab → Shop & blog), newest first, usually on a Blog page and as "Latest news" on the home page. Each post gets its own address (/blog/post-name/), a link back to the blog, and is added to the sitemap for Google. New blogs made with AI start with three posts written for the business; templates start with three drafts to fill in.

Language, privacy & cookies

These are in Site → Language, privacy & cookies.

  • Website language: chosen in the first step of a new website, or here. With AI, the whole site is written in it. It also sets the language for Google and the few words the builder adds itself (dates, "Sold out", the cookie notice). To translate an existing site, ask the AI tab, e.g. "translate the whole site into Welsh".
  • Privacy policy: every new website gets one, written from what the site actually does (its forms and their fields, a map, videos, Stripe or PayPal links, web fonts, tracking). It's linked in the footer and kept out of the menu. If you add a booking form or tracking later, click Rewrite from the site's details. It's a sound starting point for a small UK business, not legal advice, so read it through.
  • Maps and videos load only when a visitor clicks them (Google Maps and Vimeo set cookies; YouTube uses its privacy mode). So most sites need no cookie notice at all, and stay free of JavaScript.
  • Tracking code (Google Analytics, Meta Pixel…): paste it here. A cookie notice then appears, and the code only runs after a visitor clicks Accept. Their choice is remembered, and the Cookie settings link in the footer lets them change it. A Custom code block can wait for consent the same way (tick "Only run it after the visitor accepts cookies").
  • Any page can be left out of the menu: untick Show in the menu in its page settings.

Customer review & the site check

Getting the customer's feedback

In Site → Customer review, click Create a review link and send it to the customer. They see your latest draft (not the live site) and, without logging in, can press 💬 Comment, click any part of the page and say what they'd like changed, then press Approve when they're happy. Their comments appear in the same place in the editor: Show jumps to the part they mean, and ✓ Done ticks it off. You can turn the link off at any time.

The site check

When you press Publish, the builder checks for things visitors would notice: placeholder pictures, leftover template text, buttons that link nowhere or to missing pages, text that's hard to read, and a contact form with nowhere to send messages. It also suggests improvements like image descriptions and Google descriptions. Show takes you to each one. You can run it any time from the top of the Site tab.

Publishing

  • Preview shows your latest changes privately. Publish makes them live.
  • On the builder's own server, each site gets an address, and you can add the customer's domain in Site → Address.
  • On the customer's own hosting: in Site → Your hosting (FTP), enter the FTP details from their hosting control panel, click Test connection, then Save. From then on, Publish uploads the site there. Only changed files are sent.
  • Prefer to upload by hand? Download the published site as files gives you a ZIP for any web host.

Published pages are plain HTML with no JavaScript, so they load very quickly.

Online shops

Simple shop (built in)

Choose Online shop when making the site, or add a Products block (Add tab → Shop & blog). Each product has a photo, name, price, description and a button. To take payments, paste a payment link into the product's Buy link:

  • Stripe: in the Stripe dashboard, Payment links → New, set the product and price, and copy the link. Stripe handles the card payment, delivery address and receipt.
  • PayPal: a PayPal.Me link with the amount, e.g. paypal.me/yourname/25, or a PayPal "Pay" button link.
  • No link? The button goes to the Contact page instead, which suits made-to-order items.

Tick Sold out to grey a product out. This suits small shops with a handful of products; there's no basket or stock control.

Bigger shops: CubeCart

For a full shop with a basket, stock and orders, install CubeCart (free) on the hosting to run the basket, payments and orders, and design the look in EKA Builder. In Site → Online shop (CubeCart), click Download as CubeCart skin, upload the ZIP into CubeCart's skins folder, extract it, and choose it in the CubeCart admin. Instructions are in the ZIP.

EKA HQ: your AI staff

For the administrator: click HQ at the top of the Websites page. Five AI staff work in a little office and bring you their work to check. Nothing reaches a customer until you approve it (except replies you let Sam send without asking, which is off to start with). The red number next to HQ shows how many things are waiting for you. Click a desk to see just that person's work. In the office, staff sit at their desks while they have work, carry finished work over to your desk, and head to the coffee corner when there's nothing to do.

  • Sam (Support) drafts a reply to every message your customers send from the support page, using these help pages and the customer's plan and websites. Edit the reply if you like, then Send reply: it appears on the customer's support page and is emailed to them. Anything about money, refunds or their account is marked Needs a person.
  • Bea (Website builder) builds done-for-you websites. Fill in Build a website for a customer; she picks a design (or use yours), writes every page for the business, and brings you a review link and an email to send the customer. Open it in the editor to make changes first.
  • Alex (Account manager) writes a friendly check-in email for each customer with a live website in the first week of each month: visitors, unanswered messages and a suggestion or two.
  • Otto (Operations) checks every hour that backups ran, the disk isn't full, the licence is fine, email works, no website failed to build and no customer has form messages waiting days for a reply, and once a day that connected domains still point here. Alerts close themselves when the problem is fixed.
  • Mia (Marketing) writes three social media posts every Monday. Choose your own website under Your staff and she also writes a blog post for it (added as a draft when you approve) and drafts replies to enquiries sent through it.

Use Run now to have them do their rounds straight away, and untick At work to give someone time off. Without email settings, use Copy and mark sent and paste the text into your own email. Without AI, Sam and Mia leave the writing to you, and Bea builds from the design's template text.

HQ works while the builder is running, so it runs all the time on a server, but only while your computer is on if you run the builder there.

Customers, plans & control panels

As the administrator (you log in with the password from settings.txt, leaving the email empty), you see every website. Your customers log in with their email and password and see only their own websites.

Adding customers

Click Customers at the top of the dashboard, then + Add customer. Give their email and choose a plan; leave the password empty and one is made up and shown once, with a button to copy the login details. From the list you can change a customer's plan, make a New password, Edit or disable them, or delete them (keeping or deleting their websites). Open as customer logs you in as them to help with a problem; log out and back in to return.

Plans

Each plan sets how many websites a customer can have and their AI allowance: the AI cost, in US dollars at Anthropic's prices, they can use each calendar month (0 = no AI). As a guide, a new AI-designed website costs about $0.30–$1 and a change a few cents. When a customer runs out, AI stops for them until the 1st, and everything else keeps working. Customers see their plan and a "% used" bar on their dashboard.

Billing

Billing report on the Customers page downloads a spreadsheet for any month: each customer's plan, websites, AI actions and AI cost. Use it to bill AI separately or to check your margins.

One-click login from cPanel, DirectAdmin and WHMCS

The builder's tools folder has a cPanel plugin (a Website Builder icon), a DirectAdmin plugin and a WHMCS module (creates, suspends and removes customers with their orders, plus an "Open Website Builder" button). Each needs the host API address and key from the bottom of the Customers page. Step-by-step instructions are in tools/README.md. Customers who arrive this way don't need a builder password.

Licence

A new builder has a 14-day free trial. Enter your licence key in Customers → Licence (or LICENCE_KEY= in settings.txt). The builder checks it twice a day. If the licence ends, making new websites, adding customers and AI pause, but every published website stays online, and existing websites can still be edited. When a new version is out, your dashboard says so, with a link to what's new.

Backups

Everything (websites, images, form messages, customers and plans) is backed up automatically once a day, and the last 7 backups are kept (BACKUP_KEEP in settings.txt changes how many; BACKUP_DIR where). On the Customers page, under Backups: Back up now, Download a backup to keep a copy somewhere else, or Restore one. Restoring puts everything back as it was then, after backing up what's there now, so it can be undone. To move to a new server, download a backup, install the builder there, and use Restore from a file.

Installing and updating on Linux

Run sudo bash tools/install-linux.sh from the builder's folder: it sets everything up as a service and prints your administrator password. To update, run sudo bash /opt/eka-builder/tools/update-linux.sh followed by the new version's file or link. Your websites and settings are backed up first, and the old version comes back automatically if the new one doesn't start.

Forgotten passwords

Customers can click Forgot your password? on the login page for an email link, when the server can send email (see Settings). Otherwise, make them a new password from the Customers page.

Demo mode

With DEMO_MODE=1 in the settings, the login page shows Try the demo. Each visitor gets a private workspace without a password. They can do everything except publish or upload to hosting, AI use is capped, and their sites are deleted after a day.

Settings

Email for forms: on a Linux server with sendmail (most have it), form emails work straight away. Otherwise add your email provider's details in settings.txt: SMTP_HOST, SMTP_PORT, SMTP_USER, SMTP_PASS, and MAIL_FROM for the sender address.

Settings live in settings.txt (Windows) or environment variables (servers). Restart the builder after changing them.

Troubleshooting

“The password is wrong”

Check ADMIN_PASSWORD in settings.txt (no spaces around it), save, and restart the builder.

The builder window closes straight away on Windows

Make sure you extracted the ZIP first and that Node.js LTS is installed. Run start-windows.bat again and read the message in the window.

“Port in use”

The builder tries the next free port and shows the address it used. Or set PORT to another number.

FTP won't connect

Double-check the host, username and password in the hosting control panel. Some hosts need the folder set to public_html. Use Test connection to see the exact error.
